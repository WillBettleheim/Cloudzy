# Cloudzy audit and upgrade, October 2026

What was found when the whole app was audited on 2026-10-22, what was changed, and what remains. Everything
below was checked against the code, the data files and the live services, not taken from older documents.

## Findings

### Features

All of these work end to end and are covered by tests:

- Weather (Open-Meteo forecast, alerts, briefing) and History & Trends.
- Weather Explorer (GFS globe and map).
- Air Quality and Outdoor Intelligence.
- Earth Intelligence (nine disaster feeds on a MapLibre globe).
- Cloudzy Core (Delhi temperature model) with Prediction History and the Lab.
- Places, Settings and Terms.

Earth Intelligence and Air Quality are documented in their own files.

### Cloudzy Core: what the ML actually does

- **One deployed model family.** Six HistGradientBoosting regressors (one per horizon: 1, 2, 3, 6, 12 and 24 h)
  predict the change in Delhi's ERA5-Land reanalysis 2 m temperature from 25 hours of ECMWF IFS inputs.
- **Three research models.** Random forests and boosting with reanalysis inputs for Delhi, Mumbai and Reykjavík;
  offline only.
- **No other ML.** Weather, air quality, hazards and outdoor advice are provider data or documented rules. The
  registry now says so explicitly.
- **The evaluation was already sound:**
  - chronological train, validate and test years, and a monthly refit with a held-out acceptance month;
  - persistence, diurnal, linear and provider (IFS) baselines, and week-block bootstrap intervals;
  - the residual target keeps the current value as an input, and every feature uses only data at or before t.
  - No leakage was found. The split helper already drops training rows whose labels reach into the next period.

### Gaps that were found

1. **No per-prediction uncertainty.** The app showed a 2025 MAE as "±", which implied a range it didn't have.
2. **Evaluation ran only when a button was clicked or a command run.** All 108 tracked predictions were pending.
   That was correct, though: the reanalysis was published only up to 15 October (a ~6-day lag) and the first
   target was 19 October. Nothing would ever have been evaluated unless someone acted.
3. **An implausible reference value would have been scored as if real.**
4. **No single view of models:** versions, data, validation, metrics and artifact integrity were spread across
   sidecars and reports.
5. **The shell had grown to 13 pages in three groups with no landing summary.** The Weather hero's stats
   overlapped when the sidebar made it narrow.
6. Research test periods differ by model (Reykjavík's starts 2025-05-26), so their MAEs aren't comparable. This
   wasn't stated anywhere.

## What was done

| Area | Change | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Uncertainty | `src/intervals.py`: split-conformal 80% and 95% ranges per horizon, calibrated and validated on later, disjoint periods; served only for the exact bundle (SHA-256) | 80% coverage 80–89% on 2025 H2 (method check), 83–86% on September 2026 for the deployed model; 95%: 96–98%. Conservative, never too narrow. The study reproduces exactly on a re-run |
| Tracking | `invalid` state (migrated with a backup, row count verified); clearer states; interval bounds stored; range coverage and skill vs IFS in summaries; scheduled background evaluation in the server process (≤ every 6 h, only when due) | Full lifecycle test from creation to evaluated summary; migration test on an old-schema database; scheduler throttling tests; the real database migrated (108 rows kept) |
| Registry | `src/registry.py` and the Model Lab → Registry tab: every model with algorithm, parameters, sources, training ranges, validation, metrics (period and n), checksum status; non-ML systems listed apart | All 4 local artifacts verify; tests cover missing and tampered artifacts |
| Model Lab | New Registry and Uncertainty tabs; live-tracking tab shows skill and coverage; limitations corrected | Page tests |
| Weather | Cloudzy panel shows the validated 80% range (metric, error bars, table); MAE no longer shown with "±"; hero stats respond to their own width | Browser-checked |
| Shell | Overview landing page (labelled tiles, each failing independently, hazards never block); navigation regrouped (Today, Explore, Cloudzy Core, You); Weather moved to `/weather` with old links forwarded; "Prediction History" and "Model Lab" names | Overview page tests; forwarding and every new view browser-checked |
| Disaster risk | Historical seismicity for selected earthquakes: catalogue counts, rates, largest event, completeness and b-value with bootstrap interval, labelled "historical analysis, not a prediction", linked to the GEM hazard map | b-value recovers a known slope on synthetic data; live check on the Reykjanes Ridge (b = 1.38, CI 1.28–1.49) |

**No model was retrained or replaced.** Nothing in the audit showed a model problem that new algorithms would
fix. The honest open question is live accuracy, and that needs evaluated live predictions first.

## Not done, and why

- **Cloudzy-made hazard predictions (floods, cyclones, drought, wildfire, severe weather).** Not built. There
  are no labelled outcome data connected to train or validate them. Official warnings and GDACS estimates are
  shown, with their sources, instead.
- **Drift monitoring.** Needs evaluated live predictions; none exist yet (the first are due around 26 October).
  The IFS bias drift is already documented in the Lab's live-setting tab.
- **Geographic generalisation.** Cloudzy Core is deliberately limited to its Delhi grid cell. It refuses
  elsewhere rather than extrapolating.
- **A full design-system pass of every page.** The shared tokens, chips and panels from `src/theme.py` were
  reused rather than reinvented. Older pages (History, Explorer, Places) weren't restyled.
